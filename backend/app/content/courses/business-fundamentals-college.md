# What is a business?

A **business** is an organisation that provides goods or services to customers in exchange for money, aiming to earn a **profit**.

## Goods and services

- **Goods** are physical products: rice, mobile phones, clothes.
- **Services** are actions done for someone: tutoring, ride-sharing, banking, hair-cutting.

Many businesses sell both. A restaurant sells food (goods) and the experience of being served (service).

## Profit

> **Profit = Revenue − Costs**

- **Revenue:** money received from sales.
- **Costs:** money spent to run the business (materials, rent, salaries, electricity).

If costs are greater than revenue, the business makes a **loss**.

**Example:** Nadia sells home-made pitha. In one month she sells 400 pieces at ৳25 each, so revenue is ৳10,000. Ingredients, gas and packaging cost ৳6,500. Profit = ৳10,000 − ৳6,500 = **৳3,500**.

## Why businesses matter

- They create **jobs** and income.
- They pay **taxes** that fund roads, schools and hospitals.
- They solve problems and improve lives through new products.

## Sectors of the economy

| Sector | Activity | Bangladesh examples |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | extracting natural resources | farming, fishing, natural gas |
| Secondary | manufacturing and construction | garments (RMG), pharmaceuticals, steel |
| Tertiary | services | banking, mobile financial services, telecom, retail |

Bangladesh's ready-made garments (RMG) industry is one of the country's largest export earners, while services such as mobile financial services have grown rapidly.

## Stakeholders

Anyone affected by a business is a **stakeholder**: owners, employees, customers, suppliers, the government, the local community and the environment. Good businesses consider all of them, not only the owners.

## Check your understanding

1. Is a private tuition centre a goods or a service business? *(Service.)*
2. A shop has revenue of ৳80,000 and costs of ৳92,000. What is the result? *(A loss of ৳12,000.)*

# Types of business ownership

Choosing a legal form affects who controls the business, who is responsible for debts, and how money can be raised.

## Sole proprietorship

Owned and run by **one person**. Very common in Bangladesh: corner shops, tailors, small online sellers.

- ✅ Easy and cheap to start; the owner keeps all profit and makes all decisions.
- ❌ **Unlimited liability**: if the business owes money, the owner's personal savings and property can be used to pay. Limited capital; the business depends on one person.

## Partnership

Two or more people share ownership, money and work, ideally under a written **partnership deed** that sets out each partner's investment, profit share and duties. In Bangladesh partnerships are governed by the Partnership Act, 1932, and firms can register with the RJSC (Registrar of Joint Stock Companies and Firms).

- ✅ More capital and shared skills.
- ❌ Usually unlimited liability; disagreements between partners can harm the business.

## Private limited company (Ltd.)

A separate **legal entity** owned by shareholders, registered with the RJSC under the Companies Act, 1994.

- ✅ **Limited liability**: shareholders can lose only what they invested. Easier to raise money and to continue if owners change.
- ❌ More paperwork, registration costs and legal duties (annual returns, audited accounts). Shares cannot be offered to the general public.

Bangladesh also allows a **One Person Company (OPC)**, which gives a single owner limited liability.

## Public limited company (PLC)

Can sell shares to the public, often listed on the **Dhaka Stock Exchange** or **Chittagong Stock Exchange**, and is regulated by the Bangladesh Securities and Exchange Commission (BSEC).

- ✅ Can raise very large amounts of capital.
- ❌ Strict disclosure rules; owners may lose control as shares spread.

## Other forms

- **Cooperatives:** owned by members who share benefits, common in agriculture and dairy.
- **Social enterprises:** businesses whose main aim is a social or environmental goal, reinvesting profit into that mission.
- **Franchises:** the right to run a business using another company's brand and system, for a fee.

## Summary table

| Form | Owners | Liability | Raising money |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sole proprietorship | 1 | unlimited | hard |
| Partnership | 2+ | usually unlimited | moderate |
| Private limited | shareholders | limited | easier |
| Public limited | public shareholders | limited | easiest |

**Note:** Registration rules, fees and procedures change. Always confirm the current requirements with the RJSC and your local authority before starting.

# The business environment

No business operates alone. Forces outside its control shape what it can do.

## PESTLE analysis

| Factor | Questions | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **P**olitical | Is the government stable? What policies affect us? | tax incentives for an industry |
| **E**conomic | Are incomes rising? What about inflation and exchange rates? | a weaker taka raises the cost of imports |
| **S**ocial | What do people value? How is the population changing? | a young population that shops online |
| **T**echnological | What new tools exist? | mobile payments such as bKash and Nagad |
| **L**egal | Which laws apply? | labour law, food safety, consumer protection |
| **E**nvironmental | What is our environmental impact or risk? | floods disrupting supply routes |

## Competition

Every business has competitors:

- **Direct competitors** sell the same thing (two biriyani shops on one street).
- **Indirect competitors** meet the same need differently (biriyani shop vs a food-delivery app's other restaurants).

## SWOT analysis

SWOT combines the inside view with the outside view:

| | Helpful | Harmful |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal** | **Strengths** (skilled team, good location) | **Weaknesses** (little capital, no website) |
| **External** | **Opportunities** (rising demand, new market) | **Threats** (new competitor, rising prices) |

**Example: a small bakery in Rajshahi**

- *S:* family recipes, loyal customers
- *W:* only one oven, no delivery
- *O:* delivery apps, wedding-season orders
- *T:* a large chain opening nearby, flour price rises

## Using the analysis

A good strategy **uses strengths to seize opportunities** and **reduces weaknesses to defend against threats**. For the bakery: partner with a delivery app and market its family recipes as something a chain can't copy.

**Activity:** Write a SWOT analysis for a business you know: a local shop, a family business, or a brand you use.

# Finding business opportunities

Great businesses start with a **problem worth solving**, not with a product.

## Where ideas come from

1. **Your own frustrations:** "Why is it so hard to find a good tutor nearby?"
2. **Watching others:** what do people complain about, waste time on, or pay too much for?
3. **Changes in the environment:** new technology, new laws, new habits (e.g. more people paying by mobile).
4. **Skills you have:** design, coding, cooking, languages.
5. **Improving something existing:** cheaper, faster, closer, better quality, or for a group that is ignored.

## Idea vs opportunity

An **idea** becomes an **opportunity** when:

- The problem is **real and frequent**.
- Enough people have it (a **market**).
- They are **willing to pay** to solve it.
- **You** can deliver the solution profitably.

## A quick scoring tool

Rate each idea 1–5:

| Question | Idea A | Idea B |
|---|---|---|
| How painful is the problem? | | |
| How many people have it? | | |
| Will they pay? | | |
| Can I build it with my resources? | | |
| Do I care about it enough to keep going? | | |

Pick the highest total and test it before investing heavily.

## Common traps

- Falling in love with a solution nobody needs.
- Assuming "everyone" is your customer.
- Copying a business without asking why it works where it is.
- Thinking an idea must be totally new. Most successful businesses do an existing thing **better**.

## Entrepreneurial mindset

Entrepreneurs take **calculated risks**: they start small, learn fast and adapt. Failure in a small, cheap experiment is information, not defeat.

# Talking to customers

Before building anything, find out whether real people have the problem. This is called **customer discovery**.

## Who to talk to

People who match your target customer, **not** only friends and family, who tend to be polite rather than honest.

## Good interview questions

Ask about their **past behaviour**, not opinions about your idea:

- "Tell me about the last time you [had this problem]."
- "What did you do about it?"
- "What was hardest about that?"
- "Have you paid for any solution? How much?"
- "What happens if the problem isn't solved?"

## Questions to avoid

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| "Would you buy this?" | People say yes to be nice. |
| "Do you like my idea?" | Compliments are not data. |
| "Would you pay ৳500 for it?" | Hypothetical prices are unreliable. |

## Listen more than you talk

- Aim to talk 20% of the time and listen 80%.
- Take notes on **exact words** customers use; they make great marketing later.
- Look for **patterns** across at least 10–15 interviews.

## Signs of a real problem

- They have already tried to solve it (spent time or money).
- They describe it with emotion.
- They ask, "When can I get it?"

## Ethics

Explain why you are asking, keep their information private, and never pressure people.

**Example:** Tanvir wanted to build a revision app for HSC students. In interviews, students said their real problem was not *content* but *staying consistent*. He pivoted to study-streak reminders with peer groups.

# Value propositions and the Business Model Canvas

## Value proposition

A **value proposition** explains why a customer should choose you:

> *For* [target customer] *who* [need/problem], *our* [product] *is a* [category] *that* [key benefit]. *Unlike* [alternative], *we* [main difference].

**Example:** *For* university students in Dhaka *who* can't afford new textbooks, *BoiBari is* a campus book exchange *that* lets them buy and sell used books in a day. *Unlike* Facebook groups, *we* verify sellers and offer safe on-campus pickup points.

## The Business Model Canvas

A one-page plan with nine building blocks:

| Block | Question |
|---|---|
| 1. Customer segments | Who are we creating value for? |
| 2. Value propositions | What problem do we solve? |
| 3. Channels | How do we reach and deliver to customers? |
| 4. Customer relationships | How do we keep them? (personal help, self-service, community) |
| 5. Revenue streams | How do we earn money? (sales, subscription, commission, ads) |
| 6. Key resources | What do we need? (people, money, equipment, brand) |
| 7. Key activities | What must we do well? |
| 8. Key partners | Who helps us? (suppliers, delivery firms) |
| 9. Cost structure | What are the main costs? |

The right side (1–5) is about **value and revenue**; the left side (6–9) is about **efficiency and cost**.

## Testing with an MVP

A **minimum viable product (MVP)** is the simplest version that tests whether customers want your offer, for example a Facebook page taking pre-orders before you rent a kitchen. Measure what people **do** (order, pay, return), not what they say.

## Build → Measure → Learn

1. Build a small test.
2. Measure customer behaviour.
3. Learn: keep going, change direction (**pivot**), or stop.

# Market research and segmentation

## Market research

**Primary research:** you collect new data yourself through surveys, interviews, observation and test sales. It's specific to you but takes time.

**Secondary research:** you use existing data from government statistics (e.g. the Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics), industry reports, news and competitors' websites. It's quick and cheap but may be general or out of date.

**Quantitative** data are numbers ("62% of respondents shop online monthly"); **qualitative** data are opinions and reasons ("I don't trust online sellers with cash payment").

## Market size

- **TAM** (total addressable market): everyone who could possibly want the product.
- **SAM** (serviceable available market): the part you can actually reach.
- **SOM** (serviceable obtainable market): the share you can realistically win soon.

## Segmentation, targeting and positioning (STP)

**1. Segment:** split the market into groups with similar needs.

| Basis | Examples |
|---|---|
| Demographic | age, gender, income, education |
| Geographic | city vs village, division, climate |
| Psychographic | lifestyle, values, interests |
| Behavioural | how often they buy, loyalty, benefits sought |

**2. Target:** choose the segment(s) you can serve best and profitably.

**3. Position:** decide how you want customers to see you compared with competitors.

## Positioning map

Draw two axes that matter to customers, such as **price** (low → high) and **quality** (basic → premium), and place competitors on it. Gaps can be opportunities, but check that customers actually want what's in the gap.

**Example:** A juice brand finds big brands are cheap and sugary, while imported brands are healthy but expensive. It positions itself as *affordable, healthy, local fruit juice*.

**Activity:** Choose a product (e.g. coaching centres) and describe two segments in detail. Which would you target, and how would you position yourself?

@lesson The marketing mix (4Ps) | 25
# The marketing mix (4Ps)

Once you know your target customer, you design the offer using the **4Ps**.

## Product

- Core benefit, features, quality, design, packaging and brand name.
- **Product life cycle:** introduction → growth → maturity → decline. Strategies change at each stage; in maturity, firms often add new features or target new markets.

## Price

| Strategy | How it works | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Cost-plus | cost + a fixed markup | simple products, many small sellers |
| Competitive | close to competitors' prices | crowded markets |
| Penetration | start low to win customers quickly | new entrant in a price-sensitive market |
| Skimming | start high, lower later | innovative products, early adopters |
| Psychological | ৳199 instead of ৳200 | consumer retail |

Price must cover costs **and** match what customers believe the product is worth.

## Place (distribution)

How the product reaches customers:

- **Direct:** your own shop, website or Facebook page.
- **Indirect:** wholesalers → retailers → customers.
- **Online marketplaces** and delivery services.

In Bangladesh, cash on delivery is still common for online orders, which affects cash flow and the rate of returns.

## Promotion

- **Advertising:** TV, newspapers, billboards, online ads.
- **Sales promotion:** discounts, free samples, "buy 2 get 1".
- **Public relations:** news stories, events, sponsorships.
- **Personal selling:** face-to-face, common for expensive or business products.
- **Digital and social media** (next lesson).

## Extended mix for services: 7Ps

Services add **People** (staff behaviour), **Process** (how the service is delivered) and **Physical evidence** (the shop's look, uniforms, receipts, reviews).

## Consistency matters

All Ps must fit together. A premium product sold at a very low price in a run-down shop confuses customers.

**Activity:** Design the 4Ps for a new healthy-snack brand aimed at college students.

@lesson Digital marketing and branding | 25
# Digital marketing and branding

## Brand

A **brand** is what customers think and feel about your business: name, logo and colours, but also trust, quality and personality. A strong brand lets you charge more and keeps customers loyal.

Build it through **consistency**: the same look, tone of voice and quality everywhere.

## Digital channels

| Channel | Good for |
|---|---|
| Social media (Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, LinkedIn) | awareness, community, direct sales |
| Search engine optimisation (SEO) | being found when people search |
| Search and social ads | targeted reach, measurable results |
| Email and messaging | repeat customers, offers |
| Content (blogs, videos, tutorials) | trust and expertise |
| Influencers | reaching a ready audience |

## The marketing funnel

1. **Awareness:** people discover you.
2. **Interest:** they follow, read or watch.
3. **Consideration:** they compare options.
4. **Purchase:** they buy.
5. **Loyalty and advocacy:** they buy again and recommend you.

Different content works at each stage.

## Measuring results

- **Reach/impressions:** how many saw it.
- **Engagement rate:** likes, comments and shares ÷ reach.
- **Click-through rate (CTR):** clicks ÷ impressions.
- **Conversion rate:** purchases ÷ visitors.
- **Customer acquisition cost (CAC):** marketing spend ÷ new customers.
- **Customer lifetime value (CLV):** total profit a customer brings over time.

**Rule of thumb:** a healthy business needs CLV to be clearly higher than CAC.

**Example:** You spend ৳6,000 on ads and gain 40 customers. CAC = ৳150. If each customer brings ৳600 profit over a year, the ads are worth it.

## Honest marketing

Follow consumer-protection law and platform rules: don't make false claims, fake reviews or hidden charges. Ask for permission before messaging people, and protect their data.

# Operations and the supply chain

**Operations** is how a business turns inputs into outputs that customers want.

```text
Inputs (materials, labour, money, information)
   → Process (make, assemble, serve)
      → Outputs (goods and services)
```

## Production methods

| Method | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Job | one-off, custom | a tailor making a wedding sherwani |
| Batch | groups of identical items | a bakery baking 200 buns, then 100 cakes |
| Flow (mass) | continuous production line | a bottled-water factory |

## Quality

- **Quality control:** inspecting products at the end to catch defects.
- **Quality assurance:** building quality into every step so defects are prevented.
- **Total Quality Management (TQM):** everyone is responsible for quality, always improving.
- **Kaizen:** small, continuous improvements suggested by workers.

## Inventory

Holding stock costs money (storage, spoilage, cash tied up), but running out loses sales.

- **Buffer stock:** a safety amount kept in case of delays.
- **Just-in-time (JIT):** materials arrive exactly when needed. It saves money but is risky if suppliers are unreliable.

## The supply chain

All the steps from raw material to customer:

```text
Cotton farmer → spinning mill → fabric mill → garment factory → shipping → retailer → customer
```

Good supply-chain management means reliable suppliers, efficient transport and good information sharing. Bangladesh's RMG exporters, for example, depend on importing raw materials, meeting strict delivery deadlines and complying with buyers' safety and labour standards.

## Productivity

> **Labour productivity = Output ÷ Number of workers (or hours)**

**Example:** 12 workers make 1,800 shirts a day → 150 shirts per worker per day. Training, better equipment and better layouts raise productivity.

**Activity:** Map the supply chain for a cup of tea sold at a tea stall, from the tea garden in Sylhet to the customer.

@lesson Teams, leadership and motivation | 20
# Teams, leadership and motivation

A business is only as strong as its people.

## Organisational structure

- **Tall structure:** many levels of management; clear control but slow communication.
- **Flat structure:** few levels; faster decisions and more responsibility for staff.
- **Span of control:** how many people one manager directly supervises.
- An **organisation chart** shows who reports to whom.

## Leadership styles

| Style | Description | Works well when |
|---|---|---|
| Autocratic | leader decides alone | emergencies, inexperienced teams |
| Democratic | team involved in decisions | skilled, creative teams |
| Laissez-faire | team decides, leader supports | highly expert, self-motivated teams |

Good leaders adapt their style to the situation.

## Motivation

**Maslow's hierarchy of needs:** physiological → safety → belonging → esteem → self-actualisation. People are motivated by the next unmet need.

**Herzberg's two-factor theory:**

- **Hygiene factors** (pay, working conditions, job security) prevent dissatisfaction but don't motivate on their own.
- **Motivators** (recognition, responsibility, growth, meaningful work) create real motivation.

## Practical motivation tools

- Fair pay on time, and bonuses linked to results.
- Training and promotion paths.
- Recognition: thank people specifically and publicly.
- Involving staff in decisions.
- A safe, respectful workplace.

## Hiring basics

1. Write a clear **job description** (duties) and **person specification** (skills needed).
2. Advertise through job sites, universities and referrals.
3. Shortlist, interview, and test practical skills.
4. Offer the job in writing; follow labour law on contracts, working hours and wages.
5. **Induction:** help the new person settle in during their first weeks.

**Activity:** Your café has high staff turnover. Using Herzberg, suggest two hygiene fixes and two motivators.

# Costs, pricing and break-even

## Types of costs

- **Fixed costs** don't change with output: rent, salaries, loan repayments.
- **Variable costs** change with output: raw materials, packaging, delivery per order.

> **Total cost = Fixed costs + (Variable cost per unit × Quantity)**

## Contribution

Each unit sold "contributes" towards paying fixed costs:

> **Contribution per unit = Selling price − Variable cost per unit**

## Break-even point

The quantity at which **total revenue = total cost**, so there is no profit and no loss.

> **Break-even quantity = Fixed costs ÷ Contribution per unit**

## Worked example

Rahim plans a phone-accessories stall:

- Monthly fixed costs (stall rent, his salary, electricity): ৳30,000
- Average selling price per item: ৳250
- Average variable cost per item: ৳150

Contribution = 250 − 150 = **৳100**

Break-even = 30,000 ÷ 100 = **300 items per month** (about 10 per day).

If he sells 450 items: profit = (450 − 300) × 100 = **৳15,000**.

## Margin of safety

> **Margin of safety = Actual sales − Break-even sales**

At 450 items: 450 − 300 = 150 items. Sales could fall by 150 before Rahim makes a loss.

## Using break-even to decide

- Raising the price to ৳270 → contribution ৳120 → break-even 250 items.
- Cheaper supplier (variable cost ৳130) → contribution ৳120 → break-even 250 items.
- Moving to a stall with ৳40,000 rent → break-even 400 items.

## Limitations

Break-even assumes a single price, constant costs and that everything produced is sold. Real life is messier, but it's still a powerful quick check.

## Practice

A juice bar has fixed costs of ৳45,000/month, sells juice at ৳80 and has variable cost ৳50 per glass.

1. Contribution per glass? *(৳30)*
2. Break-even quantity? *(1,500 glasses)*
3. Profit at 2,000 glasses? *(500 × 30 = ৳15,000)*

# Financial statements

Financial statements tell owners, lenders and investors how a business is doing.

## The accounting equation

> **Assets = Liabilities + Owner's equity**

- **Assets:** what the business owns (cash, stock, equipment, money owed by customers).
- **Liabilities:** what it owes (loans, unpaid supplier bills).
- **Equity:** the owner's claim (money invested + profits kept in the business).

## Income statement (profit and loss)

Shows performance **over a period**, such as one year.

| Item | ৳ |
|---|---|
| Revenue (sales) | 1,200,000 |
| − Cost of goods sold | (700,000) |
| **Gross profit** | **500,000** |
| − Operating expenses (rent, salaries, marketing) | (320,000) |
| **Operating profit** | **180,000** |
| − Interest | (20,000) |
| − Tax | (40,000) |
| **Net profit** | **120,000** |

## Balance sheet

A snapshot of what the business owns and owes **on one date**.

| Assets | ৳ | Liabilities and equity | ৳ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Equipment | 300,000 | Bank loan | 200,000 |
| Inventory | 150,000 | Supplier bills (payables) | 80,000 |
| Receivables | 60,000 | **Total liabilities** | **280,000** |
| Cash | 90,000 | Owner's equity | 320,000 |
| **Total assets** | **600,000** | **Total** | **600,000** |

The two sides always balance.

## Key ratios

| Ratio | Formula | Example above |
|---|---|---|
| Gross profit margin | gross profit ÷ revenue × 100 | 500,000 ÷ 1,200,000 = 41.7% |
| Net profit margin | net profit ÷ revenue × 100 | 120,000 ÷ 1,200,000 = 10% |
| Current ratio | current assets ÷ current liabilities | (150k + 60k + 90k) ÷ 80k = 3.75 |

A current ratio above about 1.5 usually means the business can pay its short-term bills comfortably; a very high ratio may mean too much cash is sitting idle.

## Profit is not cash

A business can be profitable on paper and still run out of cash if customers pay late. That's why cash flow (next lesson) matters so much.

# Cash flow and budgeting

> "Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity, but **cash is king**."

Many profitable small businesses fail because they run out of cash.

## Cash flow forecast

A table predicting cash coming **in** and going **out** each month.

| | Jan | Feb | Mar |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cash in**: sales received | 40,000 | 55,000 | 70,000 |
| **Cash out**: materials | 25,000 | 30,000 | 35,000 |
| Rent | 10,000 | 10,000 | 10,000 |
| Wages | 15,000 | 15,000 | 15,000 |
| **Net cash flow** | −10,000 | 0 | 10,000 |
| Opening balance | 20,000 | 10,000 | 10,000 |
| **Closing balance** | 10,000 | 10,000 | 20,000 |

Closing balance = opening balance + net cash flow, and each month's closing balance becomes next month's opening balance.

## Why cash problems happen

- Customers pay on credit or late.
- Too much money tied up in unsold stock.
- Seasonal sales (e.g. Eid peaks, then quiet months).
- Unexpected costs or fast growth that needs more stock before sales arrive.

## Improving cash flow

- Ask for deposits or advance payment; encourage digital payment over cash on delivery.
- Chase overdue payments promptly.
- Negotiate longer payment terms with suppliers.
- Keep stock lean.
- Arrange an overdraft or credit line **before** you need it.

## Budgets

A **budget** is a plan for income and spending. Compare budget vs actual every month:

> **Variance = Actual − Budget**

A *favourable* variance helps profit (higher sales or lower costs than planned); an *adverse* variance hurts it. Investigate large variances: are they one-offs or a trend?

## Personal finance link

The same skills apply to your own money: track income and spending, keep an emergency fund and avoid expensive debt.

**Practice:** Build a three-month cash flow forecast for your business idea. In which month is the business most at risk?

@lesson Sources of finance | 20
# Sources of finance

## Internal sources

- **Owner's savings** (bootstrapping): no interest and no loss of control, but limited.
- **Retained profit:** reinvesting profit instead of taking it out.
- **Selling unused assets.**

## External sources

| Source | Features | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Family and friends | flexible, quick | relationships can suffer if it fails; write the terms down |
| Microfinance (NGO-MFIs) | small loans, often without collateral | interest rate and weekly repayments |
| Bank loan | larger amounts, fixed repayment | collateral, interest, a strong business plan needed |
| Overdraft / credit line | short-term flexibility | high interest if used long-term |
| Trade credit | suppliers let you pay later | missing payments damages relationships |
| Leasing | rent equipment instead of buying | total cost may be higher |
| Angel investors | individuals invest for equity, often with advice | you give up part of ownership |
| Venture capital | large equity investment for fast-growing startups | high growth expectations, loss of control |
| Crowdfunding | many small contributions online | needs strong marketing; check platform rules |
| Grants and competitions | money that may not need repaying | competitive, with reporting requirements |

## Debt vs equity

- **Debt:** you borrow and repay with interest; you keep ownership. Repayments are due even in bad months.
- **Equity:** you sell part of the business; there's no repayment, but you share future profits and control.

## Choosing finance

Ask:

1. How much is needed, and for how long?
2. What is it for? (Use long-term finance for long-term assets.)
3. What does it really cost (interest, fees, ownership)?
4. How much risk and control can I accept?

## Interest example

A ৳100,000 loan at 12% simple interest per year for 2 years costs 100,000 × 0.12 × 2 = **৳24,000** in interest. Always compare the total cost, including fees, between lenders.

## Islamic finance

Many banks in Bangladesh offer Shariah-compliant products that avoid interest, such as profit-sharing (*mudarabah*) and cost-plus sale (*murabaha*) arrangements.

# Business ethics and social responsibility

**Ethics** means doing what is right, not just what is legal or profitable.

## Common ethical issues

- **Honesty with customers:** no false advertising, adulterated food or hidden fees.
- **Treatment of workers:** fair wages paid on time, safe workplaces, reasonable hours, no child labour.
- **Suppliers:** paying on time and not abusing power over small suppliers.
- **Environment:** pollution, waste and resource use.
- **Bribery and corruption:** damage trust and the economy, and are illegal.
- **Data privacy:** protecting customers' personal information.

## A lesson from Bangladesh

The **Rana Plaza** collapse in 2013 killed more than 1,100 garment workers. It showed the human cost of ignoring safety and led to major building and fire-safety inspection programmes across the RMG industry. Buyers, factory owners, government and workers all share responsibility for safe workplaces.

## Corporate social responsibility (CSR)

CSR means a business voluntarily considers its impact on society and the environment. The **triple bottom line** measures success by:

1. **Profit**: economic value
2. **People**: social impact
3. **Planet**: environmental impact

Examples include green factories, scholarships, clean-water projects and fair-trade sourcing. Good CSR is part of how the business operates, not just a publicity campaign.

## Why ethics is good business

- Builds customer trust and brand reputation.
- Attracts and keeps good employees.
- Reduces legal and financial risks.
- Global buyers and investors increasingly demand it.

## Ethical decision test

Before deciding, ask:

1. Is it legal?
2. Is it fair to everyone affected?
3. Would I be comfortable if it appeared in the newspaper?
4. Would I accept it if it were done to me?

**Case discussion:** A supplier offers you cheaper fabric but you suspect its factory is unsafe. What do you do, and how do you balance cost and responsibility?

# Legal and tax basics for small businesses

Running a business legally protects you, your customers and your workers. This lesson gives a general overview; **always check current requirements with the relevant authority or a qualified professional**, because rules and fees change.

## Common registrations in Bangladesh

- **Trade licence:** issued by the local authority (city corporation, pourashava or union parishad) for operating a business at a location, renewed periodically.
- **Company or firm registration:** with the **RJSC** for companies and (optionally) partnerships.
- **TIN (Taxpayer Identification Number):** from the **National Board of Revenue (NBR)** for income-tax purposes.
- **BIN (Business Identification Number):** VAT registration with the NBR, required for businesses that must collect VAT.
- **Sector licences:** food businesses, pharmacies, importers/exporters, and others may need additional permits (for example from food-safety or import-export authorities).

## Taxes, simplified

- **Income tax:** paid on business profit (or on the owner's income for a sole proprietorship).
- **VAT:** a tax on sales that the business collects from customers and pays to the government.
- **Withholding tax (TDS/VDS):** in some transactions, the payer deducts tax at source.

Keep proper records of every sale and expense, issue invoices, and file returns on time to avoid penalties.

## Contracts

A contract is an agreement the law will enforce. It needs an offer, acceptance, something of value exchanged and parties capable of agreeing. Put important agreements (partnership deeds, supplier terms, rent) **in writing**.

## Employment

Labour law covers appointment letters, working hours, leave, minimum wages in some sectors, workplace safety and termination. Treat workers fairly and keep records.

## Consumer protection

Bangladesh has a consumer-rights protection law and a directorate that handles complaints about issues such as overpricing, adulteration and false advertising. Honest pricing and quality protect you legally and reputationally.

## Intellectual property

- **Trademark:** protects your brand name and logo.
- **Copyright:** protects creative work such as text, music and software.
- **Patent:** protects new inventions.

Check that your business name isn't already someone else's trademark before you invest in a brand.

**Activity:** Make a checklist of registrations and records that your business idea would need, and find which office handles each one.

# Writing a business plan (capstone)

A **business plan** pulls together everything from this course. It helps you think clearly and is often required by banks and investors.

## Structure

1. **Executive summary** (write it last; one page): the problem, solution, market, team, money needed and expected results.
2. **Business description:** mission, legal form, location, goals.
3. **Market analysis:** target segment, market size (TAM/SAM/SOM), competitors, SWOT and PESTLE highlights.
4. **Product or service:** what you sell, your value proposition, and evidence from customer interviews or an MVP.
5. **Marketing plan:** positioning and the 4Ps (7Ps for services), digital strategy, and target CAC.
6. **Operations plan:** suppliers, production method, location, equipment, quality and key milestones.
7. **Management and team:** who does what, organisation chart, hiring plans.
8. **Financial plan:**
   - start-up costs
   - pricing and break-even analysis
   - 12-month cash flow forecast
   - projected income statement
   - funding required and sources
9. **Risks and mitigation:** what could go wrong and your response.
10. **Legal and ethical considerations:** registrations, licences, CSR.
11. **Appendices:** survey results, quotes, CVs.

## Tips

- Be **realistic**: show evidence for your assumptions.
- Keep it clear and concise; 10–15 pages is plenty for a small business.
- Use tables and charts for numbers.
- Update it as you learn; a plan is a living document.

## Pitching

Prepare a 3-minute pitch: hook, problem, solution, market, business model, traction, team, and the ask (how much money or help you need). Practise answering tough questions about your numbers.
